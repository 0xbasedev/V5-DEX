# Handoff prompt — The Machine (v16, 2026-11-10, aligned to spec v1.16)

*Numbering note: this workspace's brief v12/v13 differ from the earlier reviewed briefs with the same numbers (spec O2).*

We are designing a new chain-agnostic exchange. Build to this brief, and flag anything you think is wrong rather than silently changing it. A full spec (v1.16) governs. It marks every item LOCK, PROPOSAL, or OPEN, and its §18 records the rulings of 2026-11-10. Where the spec and this brief differ, the spec wins. Where the spec is silent, ask instead of inventing numbers. Every cap, share, date and rate below is OPEN until a simulator run supports it.

**v1 scope (LOCK):** V5 pools, the Epoch Hall, a stateless router, the V5 auto-compound vault and the app, on EVM only. Later phases are each gated on a measurable trigger (OPEN): our hook suite on Uniswap's canonical v4 PoolManager; V2, V3 and weighted engines under the same shell; compounders for other protocols' farms; the token, ve-locks and gauges; and Solana at SDK parity. V4 means our hooks on Uniswap's deployment, never a fork of v4-core, which is BUSL-licensed until the earlier of 2027-06-15 or a governance-set date.

**V5:** the swap curve is a weighted or stable-amplified pool, and the LP share is a Masonry-style position. Receipts season in the Epoch Hall on a linear curve with a floor, and are paid every 6-hour epoch from REAL fees, never printed tokens. A position's age travels with it when it is transferred. New capital opens a new position at age zero, and only mature positions may merge. Compounded fees join at the position's current age, so compounding never resets seasoning. Only unlocked fees can be compounded, and fees from outside farms count as new money. Fees earned while a position seasons are held in the Hall and unlock on the same curve, fully at maturity. The app shows "Earned / Unlocked" with a filling bar.

**Exit:** there is one `exit()` and one price. The cost depends only on tier and age and melts to zero at maturity. It never reads live pool state. On the soft tier, leaving early forfeits only the still-locked fees, with at most a small anti-churn principal floor. The hard-lock tier pays a heavier early-exit cost, and "panic exit" is the same function applied to it. Forfeits stay in the pool as depth: a ruled share becomes POL, and the rest goes to seasoned weight. Exit costs are waived on a guardian pause or a time-weighted depeg measured against an external reference. Deposit caps can only be raised, and pause never blocks exit. Peg pools run the tuning-fork directional fee, with the pitch taken from config or an external oracle and never the pool's own TWAP.

**Fees:** the gross fee is competitive from day one, with a small venue cut inside it. Step-ups follow a code-capped schedule published at launch. The venue cut is split into the build fund, POL, buyback, ve share, the prize slice, the boost budget and the integrator share, and the split sums to 100%. Partners may add their own fee, capped in the bounds table and quoted separately.

**Token (later phase):** fixed supply and deflationary only. There is **one token, on a home chain** (home chain OPEN). Other chains swap their home-bound share to USDC, with a slippage guard, and send it home over CCTP, Circle's cross-chain USDC transfer. The transport is replaceable under the longest timelock. The token never moves cross-chain. **In v1 every chain banks its share locally, and the home chain is picked later (Jess ruling).** **Frozen at v1, on each launched chain:** a local escrow that only holds, releases at a capped percentage per epoch to one fixed address (the outlet), pauses, and refunds its own pools by deferred claims. The timelocked deploy contract that derives the outlet's CREATE3 address is frozen too. The escrow releases nothing until the token exists, and only once the code at the outlet address matches the hash published at queue, so before launch every banked dollar stays in frozen v1 code. It has no setter, no transport, no chain list and no domain write. **Written later, once the home chain is known:** the outlet. It moves USDC home (or feeds the Engine on the home chain) and goes through the same timelock, watcher and queued-change freeze as the Engine. CCTP and transport choices are made then. Any chain, including one launched after v1, can be home. **Pre-token split, 50/50 (Jess ruling):** half of each pre-token buyback share goes straight into that pool's normal fee ledger in the Hall, in the tokens collected, and never enters the escrow. That routing lives in immutable pool/Hall code. The fee splitter only decides how much of the other half goes to the escrow, where it is swapped to USDC and banked for the launch burn. The split is fixed at deploy, and the pre-token buyback line can only go down. The banked half stops at a per-chain holding cap (amount OPEN), and anything above it goes to LPs the same way. USDC with no ledger entry is never credited to a pool. Nothing is released while an Engine or outlet deploy is queued or during its first epoch live. Banked amounts are recorded each epoch as deferred claims in the Hall, weighted by seasoning in that epoch, and paid only when refund USDC arrives, so whatever wasn't drawn is split by those weights. Claims stay with the wallet that earned them and can't be transferred. A position's accrued claim settles to its owner whenever the position is transferred, exited or merged. A refund also fires if payouts stay paused past a set period, even once the token exists. A stop-only pause is held by its own wider key set: any single member can pull it, a threshold can unpause on a timelock shorter than the refund trigger, and a threshold can remove a member at once except while an Engine or outlet change is queued, with a floor on the set's size. The stuck-pause refund period must exceed the longest timelock plus the unpause timelock. The sunset refund ignores the pause and pays each chain's own pools from its own escrow.

**Buyback Engine + Burn Vault (amends LOCK §5b):** the Engine holds stables, and the token address is committed at deploy with no setter, and the Engine buys only when the code at that address matches the audited token's code hash, checked on every buy. The token is a plain contract: no proxy, no delegatecall, no upgrade path, no owner. Its code is frozen and audited before the Engine deploys. It reads its chain id at call time, so one hash holds on every chain. The code carries no numbers: supply and allocations are storage values set once at deploy. Neither the token nor the Engine is frozen at v1 (Jess ruling). The escrow fixes only the outlet's address, and the Engine sits behind it on the home chain. Each deploys later through the longest timelock, with its code hash and every deploy-time value (supply, allocations, recipients, buy cap, price window, release rate) published when queued. Numbers are set in the constructor only, inside bounds in the code. Addresses are written into the code. The outlet's deploy contract enforces the timelock and is frozen v1 code on every launched chain. Only limits the escrow enforces are guarantees. Supply and balances are checked at mint, after which the Engine checks only the code hash and that supply is at or below the commitment. A watcher checks everything during the timelock, and the stop-only pause is the response. Remaining risk, stated: a stolen deployer key could queue a bad Engine or outlet. The loss is zero if the watcher catches it, or the release rate times the number of epochs until someone pauses, from the banked half only. This bound starts only at launch. After launch, refunds cover only what the escrow still holds. The LPs' half is never exposed. The launch burn benefits everyone holding the token at launch, and early LPs share in it through their credits airdrop. It buys in a capped, externally priced stream, with a delayed start and a long-window average while the token has no outside market. It has a sunset date fixed at deploy: if no token exists by then, each pool is paid back its banked half by its deferred claims over the whole banking period. The Burn Vault holds only the token. It has no withdraw and no admin, burns once on Burn Day, and its meter counts tokens burned.

**Gauges (post-token):** votes direct POL placement and a boost budget carved from the venue cut. Each pool's cap scales with its own fees, plus one fixed flat slice for new pools that pass the age, depth and oracle checks. There are never emissions.

**Credits and Bonfire:** the exchange launches tokenless. Trader credits accrue only on venue fees paid, and LP credits on dollars × time. Credits never transfer with a position. The monthly Bonfire is free to enter. Prizes come from the prize slice of fees and are split by seasoned weight integrated over the month. No credit multipliers, and nothing that multiplies earnings or credits is ever sold.

**Developer surface:** an SDK and API at parity with the venues developers already integrate, copy-paste snippets, zaps and LP-position swaps through the stateless router, and immutable router versions. Permits are per-transaction through canonical Permit2. Canonical addresses live in an on-chain registry, and snippets verify against it before asking for a signature. A zap out of a position discloses its exit cost before signing. The hook registry rejects any hook with remove-liquidity or return-delta permissions, and hooks are code-hash-pinned. The MCP server builds unsigned transactions against our own router and factory only. It defaults the recipient to the signer, shows a plain-language preview, and never holds keys.

**Platform:** everything is config, in percentages with precision per spec §14.11. Deployments are deterministic with CREATE3, at the same addresses on every standard EVM chain. New chains are entered on testnet before mainnet. Cores are immutable. The fee-config registry is the only mutable surface: code-bounded, timelocked and per-chain. A guardian may pause and waive but never set values.
