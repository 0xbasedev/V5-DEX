## 3. V5 — Masonry liquidity (the tuning fork)

- **Base curve:** weighted pool math (proven). All novelty lives in the **share accounting** — the LP share is a Masonry position.
- **Curve modes (PROPOSAL, added v1.2 on review):** weighted math has high slippage near a peg, which collides with the §3 whale pitch for stable pools. V5 therefore needs **two curve modes**: *weighted* (general pairs) and *stable-amplified* (a StableSwap-style amplified invariant for peg pairs). The whale stable product runs on the amplified mode; the seasoning/toll layer is identical across modes.
- **The three Masonry primitives, ported (PROPOSAL — mechanics presented 20:24–20:33, framing endorsed, parameters not yet ruled):**
  1. **The receipt ages.** LP receipts stake in the Epoch Hall (§4); position age is tracked (weighted average). **[→ §18.9: age travels with the position; new capital opens a new position; only mature positions merge.]**
  2. **Epochs pay the aged.** Every 6h epoch, collected fees distribute to staked receipts pro-rata by seasoned weight. **The distribution is real fee revenue — never printed tokens.** No mint means no death spiral.
  3. **Exits melt.** **[→ §18.3: exit cost is a published function of tier and age only; seasoning fees are held in the Hall and the soft tier forfeits those first.]** Leaving early pays an exit toll **in LP**, which stays in the pool as POL (the impatient pay the patient, permanently). The toll decays with seasoning and reaches **0% at maturity**. Working example only (NOT ruled): 6% toll, 4-week horizon.
- **LOCK — Tuning fork (Jess, 20:26):** "V5 will operate sort of like a tune fork." The pool holds a reference **pitch** (peg TWAP / target weights, in config). Swaps pushing price **toward** the pitch are cheapest; swaps pushing **away** are damped harder the further the detune (the Fire Tax curve as a detune function). Arbitrageurs keep striking the fork; the pool skims energy from every strike into POL. The melting exit toll is the sustain: hold the note longer, exit purer. Directional fee ships config-gated, off by default per pool.
- **Pitch source (PROPOSAL, added v1.2 on review — the spec previously never said what supplies the pitch):** the pitch is set **in config at pool creation** — a fixed reference price (e.g., 1.00 for a stable pair) or the platform oracle's price — and is **never the pool's own TWAP alone**: a pool-TWAP pitch drifts, and a long depeg would silently become the new pitch. Re-pitching is a deliberate, visible Admin act. The directional fee **split between LPs and POL is a config field** (PROPOSAL; Jess's Fire Tax 50/50 split is the standing precedent, not a rule here).
- **LOCK — Seasoning rule:** LP must mature in the hall or pay. Full fee weight and the free exit unlock only at maturity.
- **Combined-entry constraint (added v1.2 on review):** a fresh LP starts near zero on the inverted curve AND faces the full exit toll — the two penalties stack at entry. Design requirement: the curve climb and the toll melt run on **one shared seasoning clock**, and for the unlocked tier that clock's horizon is **short — weeks, not months** (config). This upgrades §16 flag 3 from a preference to close to a requirement.
- **The whale consequence (Jess, 20:57):** in a V5 stable pool, just-in-time liquidity arrives unseasoned and earns ~nothing, so parked capital is never diluted by hot money — tolls, fee share, and bribes all flow to the seasoned. Incumbent DEXs pay hot money and parked money the same rate; V5 pays parked money more. Combined with the deflationary-only token (no emissions bleed under the yield) and the tuning-fork fee compensating depeg-direction risk, stable pools become the whale product.
- **LOCK — Inverted reward curve (Jess, 21:02).** Time-weighted rewards on an **inverted curve**: the rate starts LOWER and rises the longer the position seasons — the reverse of front-loaded emissions curves that pay mercenaries most on day one. LP yield is multi-asset: **protocol fees + slash proceeds + fee-driven exchange token** (buyback-acquired token distributed to seasoned LPs alongside the burn, split in config) — LPs accumulate several valuable things at once. **The app visually shows the multiplier growing** — your projected rewards as a function of staying — patience rendered as progress (gamification doctrine).
- **LOCK — Two commitment tiers (Jess, 21:02).** (a) **Unlocked pools**: Masonry-style seasoning only — exit anytime at the melting toll, rewards climb the inverted curve. (b) **Hard-lock pools**: an actual minimum lock (Jess's example: 6 months — the term is illustrative, not ruled) that earns **even more** than the seasoned-unlocked path. The ladder: wallet-held receipt (nothing) < seasoned (curve rewards) < hard-locked (top rate).
- **LOCK — Auto-compound pools (Jess, 21:05): "pools that do what Beefy does."** Auto-compounding vaults harvest the multi-asset yield and restake it into the position. Economics per Jess's existing compounder doctrine (xTOMB precedents): a convenience price — subscription per venue and/or the 0.5% performance slice taken **in LP to POL**; a Beefy-style forever percentage of yield is **not** the model (the 2% performance fee was rejected in xTOMB design). Non-negotiables: compounding **never resets seasoning** (additions blend at weighted-average age, per his standing rule) **[→ §18.9: consistent — compounded fees join at the position's current age (O1 ruled 2026-11-10)]**, manual compounding stays free forever (pay for convenience, never pay to win), and auto-compound is a natural **pay-to-open** unlock. Sequencing flag (Bingo): harvest/keeper surface phases in *after* V5 core, not in its v1.
- **LOCK — Compounders cover OTHER protocols too (Jess, 21:06).** The vaults auto-compound external venues' farm/LP positions, Beefy-style — a standalone product and the exchange's top of funnel: users arrive to compound positions they already hold elsewhere, and land inside our app, earning credits, next to our pools. Flags (Bingo): an external vault inherits the underlying protocol's contract risk (mitigation: vetted whitelist, per-vault risk labels — Beefy's own per-vault safety score is the model — non-custodial wrappers); each venue is a bespoke adapter (config-driven adapter pattern; phasing: our pools first, top external venues second).
- **Beefy adoption — the honest version (checked 2026-11-09):** Beefy's vault and strategy contracts are open source on GitHub and verifiable on-chain, and the pattern (one strategy per farm: claim → swap to underlying → re-deposit) is public and simple — adopting the architecture and reading their strategies as reference is a genuine shortcut. But "immediately adopt all their pools" does not survive contact: each strategy is welded to one farm's live contracts, reward tokens, and swap routes, and strategies rot — Beefy continuously retires vaults for exactly this reason (~1,150 active across 25+ chains, with a large retired tail). Correct play: cherry-pick living top venues; per vault, verify (a) the license on the specific component, (b) the farm's *current* contracts and routes, (c) the strategy against them — then list. Benchmark: Beefy's performance fee is ~4.5%; our 0.5%-in-LP-to-POL undercuts it by design.

## 5. The DEX token and locks

- **LOCK — Fixed supply, minted once. DEFLATE ONLY (Jess, 20:40): "we only deflate not inflate."** Nothing anywhere in the platform prints — no emissions, no gauge-directed mint, ever.
- **LOCK — ve-lock model.** Convert/lock the DEX token into a **receipt token**; choose a lock length; longer lock = more weight = more fee share. (The standard exchange model, per Jess.)
- **LOCK — Early exit allowed, for a fee.** Most ve exchanges forbid early exit; **we allow it**, priced off remaining lock time. The fee benefits the system: part to the lockers who stayed, part burned. (Production-validated pattern: Shadow's xSHADOW instant exit at a discount.)
- **LOCK — Credits → airdrop.** Users of Jess's protocols earn **credits** toward **[→ §18.6: trader credits accrue only on venue fees paid; credits never transfer with a position]** an airdrop of the DEX token at its launch. Guardrails (Bingo, stated 20:24): credits non-transferable, weighted by **dollars × time** (never raw action counts — sybil), snapshot/Merkle at launch. **Developers who integrate early also earn credits** (stated 20:32). The airdrop pays from a **fixed allocation** of the fixed supply; unclaimed airdrop **decays to burn**.
- **Two instruments, two curves (from Jess's JLP observation, 21:09).** The platform holds both animals: (a) **basket/LP receipts** (weighted pools, V5) — asset-backed, fee-fed, JLP-style slow grind; JLP is already on Jess's blue-chip Genesis list for exactly this property — with the asterisk that JLP-type instruments are the counterparty to trader PnL; (b) **the DEX token** — expectation-backed, deflationary; it trends with the venue's success but re-rates like any expectation asset. Jess's stated expectation for the token (21:11, aspiration, not a design parameter): up fast at first (token-last launch into existing fees + credits overhang), then sloping off on a natural curve like Bitcoin. Design controls supply/demand pressure, never price; the late curve is real only if demand keeps growing.
- **LOCK — Tokenless first phase (Jess, 20:49).** The exchange launches and runs **with no protocol token at all — only credits**. Credits accrue from **both sides of the marketplace**: (1) participating in our protocols, and (2) **launching your protocol's liquidity on the exchange**. The DEX token arrives later, into an airdrop snapshot backed by months of real dollars × time history instead of a two-week farm window. Until the token exists there is no price to defend, nothing to farm-and-dump, and the venue must win on utility alone.

## 15. Funding — the problem and the map (Bingo's mapping, 2026-11-09; options, not rulings)

Jess is right on both counts: a Genesis (the tomb mechanic) does not fit an exchange launch, and exchanges are capital-hungry. The three real cost buckets: **audits** (two minimum, staged per engine), **POL seeding** per chain, and **build/run** (SDK, docs, infra). Sources that fit his doctrines, in order of fit:

1. **His own protocols' flows.** The staggered launch cadence generates recurring fee events (deposit fees, tolls, slash-to-POL conversions). A config slice of protocol fees earmarked as the exchange build fund makes the protocols pay for their own venue — the flywheel: protocols fund the exchange, the exchange makes each next launch cheaper.
2. **Chain grants and ecosystem incentives.** New chains pay for day-one infrastructure; the testnet pre-positioning doctrine (§1) is also a funding strategy — arriving early with the turnkey DEX is exactly what ecosystem funds and fee-sharing programs (e.g., Sonic FeeM) exist to buy. Grants for flagship infra routinely cover audit costs.
3. **Platform fees from day one.** Tokenless does not mean revenue-less: swap fees are paid in the traded assets. A config slice funds build/POL from real usage.
4. **Capital-efficient POL.** Weighted pools (80/20) and gauge-directed POL placement mean the venue needs far less owned capital for useful depth; bribes bring external protocols' money to deepen it.
5. **Credits as deferred compensation.** Users, developers, and liquidity-launching protocols work now for a fixed-allocation future airdrop — zero cash cost today, zero inflation later (deflate-only).
6. **Later, priced by proof (Jess's call, unproposed):** at token launch, a straight public sale of part of the fixed supply — not a Genesis, a sale of a finished product with a credits history behind it.

Staging discipline: audit in phases with the engines (§13 sequencing) so each phase's cost is funded by the previous phase's flows and grants, not raised up front against a promise. Governing principle, in Jess's own doctrine: **pay from flows, never from guarantees.**

2. **Buyback Engine + Burn Vault (amends LOCK §5b item 1).**
   - Two contracts. The **Buyback Engine** holds stables and buys the token in a continuous stream. The **Burn Vault** holds only the token: no withdraw, no admin, no voting weight, one permissionless time-gated burn on Burn Day; the meter counts tokens burned.
   - The token address is **committed at deploy** (CREATE3-derived from a fixed deployer and salt). There is **no setter**. Because a CREATE3 address commits to the deployer and salt but not the code, the Engine also **hard-codes the audited token's code hash** and refuses to buy until the code at that address matches it, checked on **every buy**, not once. On a home chain without CREATE3 the address cannot be committed this way; that case needs its own rule (OPEN). The code-hash check applies in every case.
   - **The token is a plain contract:** no proxy, no delegatecall, no upgrade path, and no owner or privileged role (a hash on a proxy would pin only the proxy).

The token reads its chain id and computes its permit domain **at call time**, never storing them in code, so one code hash holds on every chain. The hash is still confirmed by a rehearsal deploy at the real address. **The code carries no numbers:** total supply and allocations (including the credits airdrop pool) are plain storage values set once at deploy (Solidity `immutable` values would change the hash, so they are not used for these). The token can never mint after deploy.

After launch, the stuck-pause refund covers only what the escrow still holds. Money already released depends on the later-written contracts, and the loss there is bounded by the release rate times the epochs until someone pauses, from the banked half only.
